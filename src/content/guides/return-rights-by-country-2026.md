A store's return policy is a promise the store makes. The law is a separate layer underneath it, and it changes a lot from one country to the next. In the UK and the EU you have a legal right to cancel most online orders. In the US, Canada and Australia, change-of-mind returns are up to the store. In Pakistan, most fashion brands only offer exchanges.

This guide explains the legal floor in each country we cover. It is a plain-language summary, not legal advice, and every section links to the official government or regulator page (checked November 13, 2026).

## United States: the store's policy is the rule

There is no general federal right to return an item you simply changed your mind about. That is why US windows range from no fixed limit at [Costco](/stores/costco) to 14 days at [Apple](/stores/apple). The FTC's guidance says that once a seller accepts a return, most refunds should be made within 7 business days, depending on how you paid. The Cooling-Off Rule gives 3 days to cancel certain sales made at your home or at a temporary location. See all [US store policies](/countries/united-states).

## United Kingdom: 14 days online, 30 days for faults

UK law gives you 14 days after delivery to cancel most online and phone orders, then 14 days to send the item back. If something is faulty, the Consumer Rights Act 2015 lets you reject it for a full refund within 30 days. Shops don't have to accept in-store change-of-mind returns, but most do: [Next](/stores/next) and [ASOS](/stores/asos) give 28 days, [John Lewis](/stores/john-lewis) 30, [Boots](/stores/boots) 35 and [IKEA UK](/stores/ikea-uk) a full year. See all [UK store policies](/countries/united-kingdom).

## Germany and France: the EU rules

Across the EU, online purchases come with a 14-day right of withdrawal from the day you receive the goods, and defective products have at least a 2-year legal guarantee. Shops add more on top: [Zalando](/stores/zalando) and [OTTO](/stores/otto) give 30 free days, and [Decathlon France](/stores/decathlon-fr) up to 365 days for account holders. In-store purchases have no legal return right, so [MediaMarkt](/stores/mediamarkt) only takes them back from card members. See [Germany](/countries/germany) and [France](/countries/france).

## Canada and Australia: the store decides, faults are covered

Canada's Office of Consumer Affairs is clear that businesses don't have to accept returns of items that aren't defective. Australia is similar for change of mind, but the ACCC adds two important points: a store with a change-of-mind policy must follow it, and for a major fault you can choose a refund or a replacement. "No refunds" signs are unlawful there. See [Canada](/countries/canada) and [Australia](/countries/australia).

## India: the product page is the policy

Indian platforms set return windows per category. [Amazon India](/stores/amazon-in) often gives 7 days, [Nykaa](/stores/nykaa) 15 and [Tata CLiQ](/stores/tata-cliq) 30 for apparel, while some electronics are replacement-only. If a company won't resolve a problem, the National Consumer Helpline (1915) takes e-commerce complaints, and unresolved cases can go to a Consumer Commission. See all [India store policies](/countries/india).

## Pakistan: exchanges, short deadlines

Pakistani fashion brands mostly offer exchanges or store credit instead of cash: [Outfitters](/stores/outfitters), [Limelight](/stores/limelight) and [Gul Ahmed](/stores/gul-ahmed) all work this way. Deadlines can be very short, such as 3 days at [J.](/stores/junaid-jamshed). For a defective product in Punjab, send the seller a 15-day legal notice, then you can file in the Consumer Court within the next 15 days, with no court fee. See all [Pakistan store policies](/countries/pakistan).

## UAE and Saudi Arabia: ask to see the policy

In the UAE, Federal Law No. 15 of 2020 requires stores to display their return policy in Arabic and English, and defective goods must be repaired, replaced or refunded. Change-of-mind returns depend on the store, and many want items unopened. In Saudi Arabia, the Ministry of Commerce says a store with a return policy must follow it, and a store without one may not refuse returns or exchanges. See [UAE](/countries/uae) and [Saudi Arabia](/countries/saudi-arabia).

## Same brand, different rights

Because the law is different, the same brand can have very different rules. [Apple](/brands/apple) gives 14 days in the US and UK but accepts no change-of-mind returns in India. [IKEA](/brands/ikea) takes back opened furniture in the UK but only unopened items in the UAE. [Amazon](/brands/amazon) ranges from 30 days in the US, UK and Canada to 15 in the Gulf.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have a legal right to return something I changed my mind about?

Only for online and distance purchases in the UK and the EU, where you get 14 days. In the other countries we cover, change-of-mind returns depend on the store's own policy.

### What is the 14-day rule?

In the UK and the EU, you can cancel most online orders within 14 days of receiving them without giving a reason. Some items are excluded, such as unsealed hygiene products and custom-made goods.

### What if a product is faulty?

Faulty products are covered by the law in every country we cover, separately from the store's change-of-mind policy. The remedy (repair, replacement or refund) and the deadlines depend on the country.

### Where can I complain if a store refuses a refund?

Contact the store first, then the official consumer body linked in the table above, such as the FTC in the US, the National Consumer Helpline in India or the Ministry of Economy (800 1222) in the UAE.
