## Quick Answer

Under US consumer protection laws (including CA, NY, TX, and FL), retailers are legally allowed to issue store credit instead of cash refunds if their return policy is clearly posted at the point of purchase.

## Key Policy Facts

| Policy Metric | Details | Status |
|---|---|---|

| **California Posted Notice Mandate** | Must post at register if policy < 7 Days | yes |
| **Store Credit Expiration Rules** | Cannot expire in CA, NY, FL | yes |

## Can I legally demand a cash refund without a receipt?
No. Retailers are legally entitled to limit receiptless returns to store credit.

## What is the legal policy for defective electronics?
Under the **Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act**, if a merchant sells a defective product, consumers are entitled to a full cash/card refund, repair, or replacement.

## Do restocking fees violate consumer laws?
No, as long as restocking fees are disclosed prior to purchase.

## How are gift store credits handled legally?
Gift cards and merchandise return cards cannot expire in states like CA, NY, and FL.

## What happens if a merchant changes its policy after purchase?
The policy in effect at the exact timestamp of purchase governs the transaction.


## Step-by-Step Return Instructions

1. **Inspect Posted Return Signage**: Check if the store posted return terms clearly at checkout.
2. **Cite Defect if Applicable**: If the item is broken, state that you are claiming an implied warranty refund.
3. **Escalate to Customer Relations**: Request store manager intervention or file a credit card chargeback if misrepresented.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a store force me to accept store credit for a defective item?
No. Federal implied warranty laws require a full refund to original payment if an item is fundamentally defective upon purchase.
