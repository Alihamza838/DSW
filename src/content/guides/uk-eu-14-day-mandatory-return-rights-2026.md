If you buy online, by phone or by mail order in the UK or the European Union, the law gives you a cooling-off period: you can cancel most purchases within **14 days** of receiving the goods, without giving a reason. This right exists on top of whatever the store's own return policy says, and the store can't take it away.

In the UK the rules come from the [Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/pdfs/uksi_20133134_en.pdf); in the EU, the [right of withdrawal explained on Your Europe](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/consumers/shopping/returns/index_en.htm). The details below are a plain-English summary, not legal advice.

## The three 14-day clocks

1. **14 days to cancel.** The period starts when you (or someone you nominate) receive the goods. If one order arrives in several deliveries, it starts when the last item arrives.
2. **14 days to send it back.** After you tell the seller you're cancelling, you normally have 14 days to return the goods.
3. **14 days to refund you.** The seller must refund you within 14 days of getting the goods back, or of receiving proof that you sent them, whichever comes first.

The refund covers the price and the standard delivery charge you paid to receive the item. If you chose a more expensive delivery option, the seller only has to refund the cost of standard delivery.

## Who pays to send it back?

Usually you do, if the seller told you so before you bought. If the seller didn't tell you that you would pay return postage, or offers free returns, you don't pay. Many stores also go beyond the law: [Amazon UK](/stores/amazon-uk), for example, offers a 30-day returns window for most items in addition to the 14-day legal right.

## Can the seller reduce the refund?

The seller can deduct an amount if the item lost value because you handled it more than you would need to in a shop to see what it is, how it works and whether it fits. Opening the packaging to inspect the item is normally fine.

## What's excluded

The right to cancel does not apply to some purchases, including:

- Goods made to your specifications or personalised.
- Goods that deteriorate or expire quickly, such as fresh food.
- Sealed audio, video or software that you unsealed.
- Sealed goods that aren't suitable for return for health or hygiene reasons once unsealed.
- Digital content, if you agreed that the download or streaming could start during the cancellation period.
- Hotel bookings, travel tickets and dated event bookings.
- Goods bought from private individuals rather than businesses.

## The legal right vs the store's return policy

These are two different things:

| | Legal 14-day right | Store's return policy |
|---|---|---|
| Who sets it | The law | The store |
| Applies to | Online, phone and mail-order purchases | Whatever the store says (often in store too) |
| Length | 14 days from delivery | Often longer, e.g. 30 days |
| Can the store remove it? | No | The store can change its own policy |

So a store can offer more than 14 days, but not less for online orders. [Amazon UK's](/stores/amazon-uk) page reflects both: a voluntary 30-day policy and the statutory 14-day right to cancel.

## Buying in a physical shop

The 14-day cooling-off period covers distance purchases. In a physical shop, you don't have an automatic right to return something you simply changed your mind about; the store's own policy decides. You do always have rights for faulty goods: in the UK, the Consumer Rights Act 2015 gives a short-term right to reject faulty goods within 30 days for a full refund.

## Buying from non-UK or non-EU platforms

When you order from a platform based elsewhere, the seller still has to respect consumer law if it targets UK or EU customers, but enforcing it can be harder. Check the platform's own returns page for your country: [SHEIN](/stores/shein), for example, lists different windows for different markets (45 days in the UK), and [AliExpress](/stores/aliexpress) offers free returns on badged items in selected countries, including the UK.

## How to cancel an online order

1. Tell the seller clearly, within 14 days of delivery, that you are cancelling. Use the store's returns page, email or the model cancellation form if one is provided.
2. Send the goods back within 14 days of telling them, and keep proof of postage.
3. Expect the refund within 14 days of the seller receiving the goods or your proof of return.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have 14 days to return anything I buy online in the UK?

For most online purchases from businesses, yes: 14 days from delivery to cancel, then 14 days to send the goods back. There are exceptions, such as personalised items and unsealed hygiene products.

### Does the 14-day rule apply to items bought in a shop?

No. The cooling-off period is for online, phone and mail-order purchases. For shop purchases, the store's own policy applies, plus your rights if the item is faulty.

### Does the refund include delivery costs?

It includes the standard delivery cost you paid to receive the item. You usually pay the cost of sending it back, if the seller told you so before you bought.

### Is the EU rule the same as the UK rule?

They are very similar: 14 days to withdraw from most online purchases and a refund within 14 days. The UK rules have been separate since the UK left the EU, so check the source for your country.
