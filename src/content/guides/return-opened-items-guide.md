You opened the box, tried it, and it isn't right. Whether you can still return it depends less on the store's window than on one word in its policy: **unopened**. Here is how the rules for opened items really differ, store by store.

## Three kinds of open-box policy

1. **Opened is fine.** The store takes it back within the window, sometimes with a smaller refund.
2. **Opened costs you.** A restocking fee or deduction applies.
3. **Sealed only.** Opened items can't come back for a change of mind.

## The same brand, different open-box rules

IKEA is the clearest example: assembled furniture is fine in the US (180 days) and the UK (refund card), but IKEA UAE only takes back unopened items. Sephora is similar: Canada accepts gently used products, while the UAE wants them unopened. Always check the page for the country you bought in.

## Hygiene items are a special case

Even generous stores exclude opened hygiene products. Boots excludes opened cosmetics and medicines unless faulty, and Chewy asks for products to be unused and unexpired. In the UK and EU, sealed hygiene items also lose the 14-day online cancellation right once unsealed.

## How to open things safely

1. Open the packaging carefully along the seams and keep every piece.
2. Test electronics for a day or two, then decide.
3. Don't register warranties or accounts on a device you may return.
4. If a fee applies, compare it with the cost of keeping or reselling the item.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I return something I opened?

Often, yes, within the window, but some stores charge a restocking fee and others only accept sealed items. Check the store's page.

### Can I return assembled IKEA furniture?

In the US within 180 days and in the UK within 365 days (as a refund card). IKEA UAE only accepts unopened items.

### Do faulty opened items have a fee?

Restocking fees are for change-of-mind returns. Faulty products are handled separately under the store's warranty and consumer law.
